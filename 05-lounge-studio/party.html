From the first yes

The shape of a party

In the order it happens

The plan, the arrival, the turn, the last hour, the strike. There are no times on this page, because the room sets those. This is what actually happens, and who’s doing what while it does.

The plan

before anyone arrives

What I’m doing
Walking the room with you, then writing one page: what the party is, how it looks, who’s doing what and what my fee is. After that, I book the suppliers, line up the crew and keep every moving part moving.
What the room’s doing
Telling me its rules: how many people, how loud, how late, what can hang from what.
What you shouldn’t worry about
The list. There is always a list, and it’s my list.

If it isn’t on the page, it isn’t happening.

The arrival

nobody goes first

What I’m doing
Standing where I can see the entrance and the room at once. I’m watching for the first person who doesn’t know anyone, and I’ve already given them somewhere to go.
What the room’s doing
Its staff are set and the bar’s open, under its own licence and its own rules.
What you shouldn’t worry about
Being on time. Be ready to enjoy your own party before anyone else arrives, and leave the room to me.

A party starts at the door, not at the speeches.

The turn

when it tips

What I’m doing
Calling it. When the room’s ready I cue the food, move the music on and open up the floor, all within a few breaths of each other.
What the room’s doing
Landing the food and keeping the bar moving, on the cue we agreed at the walkthrough.
What you shouldn’t worry about
Speeches. If you’re making one, I’ll tell you when, and I’ll make sure people are holding something.

Nobody notices the turn. That’s how you know it went right.

The last hour

is the whole job

What I’m doing
Keeping the room together. The coats stay easy, the music gets better rather than louder, and nobody’s told it’s over before it is.
What the room’s doing
Deciding how it ends. The finish, the sound level and any last orders are the room’s rules, agreed before the day, and I work inside them.
What you shouldn’t worry about
Who’s still there. The people who stay are the ones you’ll talk about it with.

Anyone can start a party. The end is where I earn the fee.

The strike

then it goes back

What I’m doing
Walking the room with its manager once the crew has taken everything down: every flower, every cable, every chair back where the room had it.
What the room’s doing
Closing up. By the end it should look as though we were never there, and my crew is the reason it does.
What you shouldn’t worry about
Anything. You went home. That was the idea.

You leave with your shoes in your hand. I leave last.
