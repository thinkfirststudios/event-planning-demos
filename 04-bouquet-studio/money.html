How the money works

What the design fee is a share of

My design fee is a share of what you spend on the room. The room means the florals, the furniture, the linen and the installation. It doesn’t mean the food, the drink, the venue hire, the dress, the band or the photographer.

I charge this way because the work scales with the room. A dinner for family and a wedding with installations are very different jobs, and a fixed price would be wrong for most of the people reading this.

What the consultation is

Before anything else we have a paid design consultation. It takes me a good part of a day: I look at your room, read what you’ve sent, walk your look through the space, and write up what I’d do. You keep those notes.

I charge for it because it’s real work, and because it’s how I make sure the people I sit down with are serious. I’ll tell you the fee in my first reply, before you’ve agreed to anything.

When it’s credited

If you go ahead with me, the consultation fee comes off the design fee. If you don’t go ahead, what you paid for was the consultation and the notes.

What the deposit does

If we go ahead, a deposit secures your date. What it is and how it works are set out in my contract. I don’t summarise that part here, because the contract is the thing that counts.

What isn’t included

  • The cost of the room itself: the flowers, hire and linen are what the fee is a share of, and you pay for them on top of it.
  • Anything on my list of things I don’t do, like seating, hotels, transport and the band.
  • Venue charges of any kind.

What makes it cost more

  • Flowers that are out of season, because they have to come a long way.
  • Anything hung overhead, if the venue agrees to it: it takes more people, more time and more equipment.
  • A venue that lets us in late or wants us out early, because the same work needs more hands.
  • Distance. Travel and extra install time go into the plan, and you’ll see them written down.
