In plain words

What it costs

The fee, the room, and which is bigger

What my fee covers

One flat fee for planning and producing the party. It covers the plan, the design, finding and booking the crew and suppliers, running the day from start to strike, and taking down what we brought in. It’s agreed up front on a single page. It doesn’t rise with what you spend elsewhere, and it isn’t a share of anything.

What the room charges separately

Most rooms ask for a food-and-beverage minimum: an amount you agree to spend with them on the day. You pay that to the room directly. It has nothing to do with my fee and I don’t see any of it. I can’t quote it for you, because it’s the room’s number, and it changes with the room, the day and the time of year. I’ll help you ask for it and read what comes back.

Which one is bigger

Usually the room’s. Most people assume the opposite, and that’s why I put it on a page. If you’re working out a total, start with the room’s minimum, then add my fee.

What makes a party cost more

  • An extra room. More space to dress and staff.
  • An install day. If the room needs us in before the day of the event, that’s crew time.
  • A late strike. If the room only lets us out after hours, the crew stays with it.
  • A custom build. Anything made for your party rather than brought in.
  • Flowers and fabric by the armful. Colour is worth it, and it’s itemised on your page.

The deposit

A deposit holds your date. Its terms are in my contract, and I’d rather you read them there than a summary of them here. Anything to do with changing or cancelling a date is in the same place.

What isn’t included

  • The room itself, and anything the room charges.
  • Food and drink. That’s between you and the room.
  • Licences and permits. Those belong to the room, and to you where a permit is in your name.
  • Guest transport and accommodation.

What happens next

We walk the room, and a page arrives afterwards with what I’d do and what my fee would be.
