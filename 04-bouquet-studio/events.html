The Big Wedding

A big wedding works when one person owns the whole thing. Most of my time goes on what nobody sees: the order the deliveries arrive in, who has the keys, and what happens if it rains at the worst moment.

I take onThe budget, the venue search, every supplier, the guest logistics, the running order and the whole day on site.

You keepWho’s on the guest list, what you wear, and what gets said in the speeches.

Everyone will have an opinion about your wedding. Mine’s the one you’re paying for.

The Small Wedding

People hire me for a small wedding because they want to be at it. There’s nowhere to hide when the room is little, so the timings matter more, not less.

I take onFinding the right room, the handful of suppliers you actually need, a tight plan for the day, and me on the floor.

You keepThe paperwork with your officiant, and telling the cousins they aren’t invited.

Small isn’t easier. Everyone in the room notices everything.

Plan this one

The Long Weekend

A wedding spread over a few days is really several events and a lot of minibuses. It’s my favourite kind of work and the kind people most underestimate, so we start with the guests and work outwards.

I take onEvery gathering across the weekend, getting people between them, where they sleep, and the timings that join it all up.

You keepYour own packing, your own speech, and remembering to eat.

By the last morning everyone’s tired and happy. I plan that breakfast harder than the dinner.

The Late Party

Big birthdays, anniversaries, anything with a dance floor. A party isn’t a dinner with music. It needs a shape, and that means knowing what happens at the point when people start looking for their coats.

I take onThe room, the food and drink arrangements with the venue, the music, the timings, and someone on the door who knows what they’re doing.

You keepWho you invite, what you say, and the fight about the playlist.

Every party has a dip. I plan for it, and you won’t see me do it.

Plan this one

The Long-Table Dinner

It sounds simple and it isn’t. Everyone eats at once, everyone can see everything, and the kitchen has one chance. I spend more time with the caterer on these than on anything else.

I take onThe room, the caterer, the table itself, the service timings and the seating logistics once you’ve told me who can’t sit together.

You keepThe seating decisions themselves. I’ll draw the plan, but your aunt is still your aunt.

Seat the talkers apart. Everything good about a dinner comes from that.

Plan this one

The Garden Lunch

Somebody’s garden is a venue with no kitchen, no toilets and no plan for rain. I love it anyway. It takes more arranging than a hotel and it’s better every time.

I take onThe weather plan first, then the hire, the food, the power, the loos and the parking.

You keepThe garden itself, and being nice to the neighbours.

Outdoors is the best room there is, and I always book the tent.
