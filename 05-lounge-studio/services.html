What I doand what I won’t

You take the room. I do what’s below, and I’ll be clear about where my part stops, because that’s where most arguments in this trade begin.

The room

I design it and direct the light. What comes in, what goes out, where the bar stands, where people will end up whether you plan for it or not. You get one page saying what I’d do, not a mood board.

Most rooms need things taken away, not added.

The shape of the night

The door, the turn, the last hour, the strike. I set how the evening moves before anyone arrives, then change it on the floor when the night has other ideas.

A plan is what you depart from on purpose.

The bar

I design where it goes and how it looks, and I work out the drinks direction with the room’s own team. They hold the licence, they buy the stock and they pour.

I care what’s in the glass. I just don’t put it there.

The people

I cast the suppliers and the crew for the night. I don’t publish a list of names, because the right people change with the room, and because nobody on my crew agreed to be on a website.

A good crew is the only thing I’d fight you for.

The floor

I run the night on the floor, start to strike, and I’m the person everyone comes to so that you’re not. The strike is mine too: my crew takes down what we brought in.

If you can’t find me, it’s going well.

What I don’t do

  • I don’t serve alcohol and I don’t hold a liquor licence. The room holds the licence, the room’s staff pour, and the studio does not serve alcohol.
  • I don’t book the room for you. You take the room, and I’ll tell you honestly what I think of it.
  • I don’t run weddings. That’s a different job and it deserves someone who loves it.
  • I don’t do daytime conferences. I’d be bad at them.
  • I don’t take a cut of the bar. What you spend with the room stays with the room.

Better you hear all of this now than on the night.

Who holds what

The room holds the licence, the room’s staff pour, and the studio does not serve alcohol. I don’t buy, stock or supply alcohol, and I’m not the person responsible for what’s poured or who it’s poured for. That’s the room, under its own licence.

Numbers, noise and finish times

These are agreed with the room and the room’s rules decide. I’ll ask the questions at the walkthrough and tell you the answers, but I can’t get you an extension, a later finish or a bigger number, and neither can anyone else who isn’t the room.

Access and food

I ask every room the same questions: how someone using a wheelchair gets in and gets to the bathroom, what the kitchen can do about allergies and what it can’t, and what happens if a guest needs somewhere quiet. I’ll pass on what the room tells me. It’s the room’s answer, not my promise.
